# Proposal: Fee-Gated Automatic Gauge Registration

## Simple Summary

Subject to a one-time CurveDAO-approved controller-authority change, add a paid route for registry-authenticated Curve gauges to register with the GaugeController after trusted-factory validation, without a separate per-gauge registration vote. This requires installation of the separate DAO-controlled router specified below; it is not an existing GaugeAdder wrapper election or a permission the live controller already grants. The fee is 10,000 crvUSD and funds four weekly tranches of vote incentives for that gauge. All amounts here are gross escrow expenditure: at the independently observed pinned Votium venue, net reward plus the current platform fee equals gross, and no fixed future fee, net reward or vote count is promised. Registration starts at raw weight zero and does not award emissions; veCRV voters retain that choice.

The DAO handles monitoring, review and kills through its existing authorized processes. When a still-Active listing's gauge's own `is_killed()` flag is observed true, its entire still-unreleased escrow is confiscated to the pinned crvUSD FeeDistributor for distribution under its normal rules — conditional on the exact transfer and the mandatory safe same-transaction FeeDistributor accounting succeeding; if that cannot complete, the transaction reverts and the escrow stays retained. A kill cleared before observation is not reconstructed, and a successful confiscation stays terminal. There is no payment to a reporter, payer or deployer. This is an additional admission route, not a retirement of the DAO proposal process: that process must be preserved through the new router with updated governance targets/payloads. No existing native wrapper route is assumed.

## How it works

1. **Paid listing.** `CurveGaugeLister.listGauge` authenticates the pool/gauge/LP relationship and structural eligibility through the configured registry. It then calls `validateGauge(gauge)` on the constructor-pinned validator `0x888888c34a46d0b2A34e9204e5273208E670e784`. Only a successful, exact 32-byte canonical true result passes. A named factory alone is insufficient: the external validator applies its configured reciprocal factory/module proof, including supported distinct-LP legacy routes. Failure rejects before fee transfer or controller addition. The owner has no paid-lane exception.
2. **Escrow and incentives.** Exactly 10,000 crvUSD is held in the lister, with 2,500 crvUSD maturing at each of the four following Thursday weekly boundaries. Anyone may checkpoint. Each listing snapshots its Votium/VoteMarket endpoints and split at execution against the owner-selected prospective defaults then in effect; later owner configuration cannot redirect that record. The payer supplies no expected endpoints, split, hash, version or deadline: defaults may change after inspection, approval or submission but before execution, and an approval or simulation is not an atomic commitment to previously reviewed terms. Live adapter settings and upstream venue policies remain mutable even for frozen records; code presence and adapter `rewardToken`/`lister` checks establish interface consistency, not independent venue identity. Every funded quarter requires exact custody movement and receipts. Funding amounts are gross escrow expenditure, not net venue rewards: the independently observed net reward plus the current platform fee equals gross at the pinned Votium venue. A late tranche uses current venue timing, not retroactive votes. VoteMarket funding is asynchronous L1-to-L2 ingress.
3. **Kill and confiscation.** Gauge kills remain external DAO/gauge-administrator actions; this lister cannot kill a gauge. Its only settlement oracle is the current gauge `is_killed()` flag, never controller weight or a report. Anyone may settle a killed Active listing. Its whole unspent remainder goes directly to crvUSD FeeDistributor `0xD16d5eC345Dd86Fb63C6a9C43c517210F1027914`, conditional on mandatory safe accounting in the same transaction. If that cannot complete, the transaction reverts and the escrow remains reserved. No early-kill refund or alternative recipient exists.
4. **DAO lane and administration.** The owner-only `listGaugeNoEscrow` lane charges no fee, creates no escrow, and does not query the trusted validator. It retains registry/structural/controller checks, so otherwise valid non-factory gauges remain eligible through DAO action. Pausing affects only new paid listings. Ownership is two-step; the intended owner is the CurveDAO Aragon org. No withdrawal, rescue, sweep, arbitrary execution or escrow-migration authority is added. This lister lane is not a substitute for the DAO's independent router-to-controller proposal route, which does not acquire the lister's admission restrictions. Controller administration resides in the separate DAO-controlled router, never in the lister.

A live listing completes after four successfully funded quarters. Previously funded venue amounts are expenditure, not recoverable escrow.

## Motivation

Separate registration eligibility from emissions preference. After the one-time governance installation and authorized activation, the paid route removes the separate per-gauge registration ballot only for candidates satisfying the defined admission checks while the lister remains authorized. It does not remove the ordinary DAO proposal option, DAO revocation or protective governance, replace veCRV weight voting, or promise liquidity, votes, yield or a launch-to-emissions timetable.

Each successful paid listing pre-funds its initial vote-incentive budget. A later authorized kill forfeits the remaining budget to the FeeDistributor rather than returning it to the payer — that forfeiture is conditional: the listing must still be Active, the current `is_killed()` flag must be observed true, and the exact transfer plus mandatory safe same-transaction FeeDistributor accounting must succeed, otherwise the transaction reverts and the escrow is retained. Forfeited amounts are gross escrow expenditure; no net venue reward or completion time is promised. Protective governance remains a DAO responsibility, without a separate reporting or reward protocol.

## Current mechanism and one-time governance ask

[Curve's current whitelisting documentation](https://docs.curve.finance/protocol/gauge/whitelisting-gauge/) describes a per-gauge 7-day on-chain vote, requiring 2,500 veCRV to create. At Ethereum block `26490527` (2026-11-25), `admin()` and `future_admin()` of canonical GaugeController `0x2F50D538606Fa9EDD2B11E2446BEb18C9D5846bB` both read `0x40907540d8a6C65c637785e8f8B742ae6b0b9968`. Its [verified source](https://eth.blockscout.com/address/0x2F50D538606Fa9EDD2B11E2446BEb18C9D5846bB?tab=contract) gates `add_gauge` on `assert msg.sender == self.admin`, not a disciple/wrapper allowlist or an epoch/budget election. Both ownership-transfer steps and type/weight administration are also incumbent-admin-only. The raw controller is not being upgraded or patched; the requested change installs a new admin composition and therefore changes its effective admission policy.

Let C be that canonical controller, D the CurveDAO executor above, R a separately reviewed DAO-controlled router, and L a fresh unchanged CurveGaugeLister. R and L are role labels, not deployed-address claims. The proposal must bind the following installation:

1. **Separate router and narrow delegation.** R has fixed canonical target C and exclusive DAO governance authority D, both supplied explicitly at construction and stored as immutables: D never rotates, the deployer/factory obtains no privilege, and there is no router-owner, target-setter, upgrade or initializer framework. Only D and adders explicitly authorized by D may add through R; the initial delegated adder is the exact L. Only D may grant or revoke that capability. Further adders require an explicit DAO decision, not permissionless enrollment or an assumed legacy privilege. L and public callers receive no router administration, grant, upgrade or arbitrary-execution authority.
2. **Exact lister interface.** Construct L with immutable `controller_=R` and DAO owner D. R exposes `add_gauge(address,int128,uint256)`, `gauge_types(address)` and `n_gauge_types()` with canonical read-through to C. Additions are synchronous, forward the actual gauge/type/weight, and support immediate typed membership and whole-call rollback. Paid additions pass zero weight; DAO-free additions pass the owner's supplied weight. Both arrive at R from L, so R must not clamp all L calls to zero or infer the outer caller. A grant is the full raw-add capability: every adder D grants — not only L — may supply any controller-valid initial weight through `add_gauge`; the paid-zero rule stays in unchanged L's own entry, never a router clamp. Valid type zero and revert-on-absence are preserved; a queued proposal or shadow registry is not compatible.
3. **DAO powers retained.** Preserve the independent D→R→C addition route without imposing L's fee, validator, registry, structural or pause restrictions. Provide DAO-only callable routes to C's `add_type`, `change_type_weight`, `change_gauge_weight`, `commit_transfer_ownership` and `apply_transfer_ownership`. Raw default-argument overloads remain usable through matching router overloads or explicitly equivalent approved proposal payloads. No administrative route is granted to L or other adders. No forwarding or generic execution is added to the lister, and no generic arbitrary-target executor is required in R. Generic execution is precisely `execute(bytes)` to the immutable C only — no target parameter, no value, ordinary zero-value CALL — and its two controller ownership selectors are mediated into the same local commit/apply state machine as the typed methods with canonical encodings only, so there is no raw ownership escape from retirement (full surface: `docs/ABI.md`).
4. **Actual governance installation.** The incumbent D calls `C.commit_transfer_ownership(R)` and then `C.apply_transfer_ownership()`; both calls must originate from the incumbent D, not the pending R. The same approved governance package binds router control, deployment/configuration, the paused L, its authorized-adder grant and adapter bindings. “One-time” describes the policy installation, not a guarantee of one transaction; bind the actual ordered governance actions and batch them only where the approved executor supports it. The ordered ask is: an explicitly approved deployment operator predeploys R with constructor (C,D) and the runtime/ABI/layout/code are verified BEFORE the seating vote becomes executable; then, in this order and each with zero ETH value, S1 `D→C.commit_transfer_ownership(R)`, S2 `D→C.apply_transfer_ownership()`, S3 `D→R.grant(L,true)`; the paid lane's unpause is a separate conditional enablement (D→L.setPaused(false)) only after all installation readbacks and the operations release gates, never baked into the seating script. Before any production apply, the complete sequence — both lister lanes, the independent D route, revocation and the R→C commit/apply handback, plus interrupted-between-steps rollback and nominee replacement — must be rehearsed in an isolated fork with the selected deployment bytecode and verified again by independent review. After installation `C.admin()==R`, never L: D retains ultimate control through R rather than remaining the raw admin address.
5. **Proposal continuity and handback.** Preserve the DAO proposal process and authority by updating governance tooling/targets/payloads to D→R→C. Old D→C admin calls will fail after the transfer; inventory queued/in-flight proposals and execute before transition or explicitly reissue/migrate under DAO approval. For handback or replacement, D instructs incumbent R to call C's commit and apply steps, in that order (policy: revoke L and every other delegated adder first for an interrupted transition). Handback to D restores the direct DAO route and disables R's raw-add authority. A successful apply permanently retires R for every mutation — canonical reads survive, retirement is never cleared and there is no revival API — so a retired R must never be reseated, and if governance selects an unusable successor or strands raw administration there is no guaranteed protocol-local recovery; wrong immutable deployments, passed votes, already-admitted paid listings and existing escrow are likewise not undoable by any invented backdoor. The proof must use callable authorized paths, not impersonate L or R as EOAs. No already-seated wrapper is assumed; any independently evidenced external route must be accounted for explicitly.
6. **Revocation and product limits.** D may revoke L while retaining direct DAO registration and full administration. Revocation blocks both L admission lanes; core pause alone blocks only new paid listings. Neither removes existing permanent registrations, changes veCRV voting, kills a gauge, refunds a payer or redirects existing escrow. Controller revocation does not gate existing escrow settlement. A raw-pinned L cannot be redirected, and router replacement/handback is not a promise that its immutable endpoint keeps accepting new listings.

The router implementation is now supplied at this commit as `contracts/GaugeControllerAdmin.sol`; this document still selects only the composition specification — production router address, deployment operator, reviewed runtime binding to a deployed instance, governance proposal approval and installation remain open, and the router is an additional separately reviewed governance dependency, not one of the already reviewed shipping contracts. No production authority is inferred from a fork-admin impersonation, a mock, the name GaugeAdder or this proposal. Installation and activation remain HOLD until the selected deployment and all evidence/approval cells in the deployment and operations references are complete.

## Limits and disclosures

- The immutable validator address has mutable upstream owner/manager-controlled policies. It authenticates the configured proof, not pool safety, economic quality or an independent deployment trace. Revocation/fault stops new paid admission, not settlement of already-admitted escrow.
- Kill authority is gauge-specific. Admission does not grant it to the payer or lister and does not verify every gauge's governance route. The DAO must verify and use the real authorized route. A kill cleared before the lister observes it leaves no historical kill proof here.
- FeeDistributor settlement requires a fresh bounded-allocation cursor and successful atomic accounting. Its public checkpoint cooldown is strict and shared; third parties can repeatedly delay settlement. Funds remain in escrow on failure, with no guaranteed completion time or recipient substitution. A standalone checkpoint is not a repair for already omitted stale allocations. If the immutable pinned FeeDistributor itself becomes permanently unusable, even a legitimately killed listing cannot terminalize: its entire outstanding remainder — the whole unspent balance — stays reserved indefinitely, backed but unavailable, until legitimate upstream restoration. No alternate FD, refund, migration, partial payout, timeout exit or owner withdrawal exists, and monitoring does not promise restoration.
- Either venue's failure rolls back the whole checkpoint. Permanent failure or incompatibility of a required immutable ingress can leave the ENTIRE affected remaining balance reserved indefinitely — including the healthy leg of a mixed listing under atomic settlement; new defaults do not repair old snapshots. A legitimate gauge kill can use the separate venue-free confiscation path, still subject to FeeDistributor accounting, so a kill is venue-free but not a guaranteed terminal exit. No refund, migration, healthy-leg-only payout, delayed-resolution machinery or kill-for-repair policy exists, and protective kills must remain independent of settlement.
- The execution-time admission race is disclosed above: reviewed deployment/configuration pins and governance remain a trust dependency, and a payer who requires enforceable, committed terms chosen before submission is not offered that guarantee by this product.
- VoteMarket L1 ingress does not establish destination campaign delivery. A funded submission whose message never creates or funds a live destination campaign still retires the local share: the entire submitted VoteMarket amount may be unavailable for its intended distribution with no restored listing liability — distinct from synchronous ingress failure, which rolls back the whole call, and from post-creation residual forfeiture. Delivery is never inferred from L1 success, adapter address equality or elapsed time; funded submissions are reconciled against actual destination evidence and unresolved spent amounts are disclosed. Unused destination rewards have no ordinary project-controlled recovery API and may equal the whole campaign funding. They do not return to listing escrow or necessarily reach Curve's FeeDistributor. See the operations reference.
- A successfully funded Votium share is final gross escrow expenditure, not proof that its incentive was processed or that voters received rewards. The venue records the adapter as depositor, but this adapter cannot withdraw or recycle an unprocessed incentive: such amounts can remain unavailable for their intended distribution indefinitely, with no project-controlled recovery and no restoration of listing escrow or payer/Curve FeeDistributor credit. Under the referenced venue policy the Votium owner can recycle an eligible unprocessed incentive; that is an independent upstream power, not a right of the lister or adapter owner and not a promise of recovery or eventual distribution. Ingress, venue processing/allocation, transfer to the venue distributor and downstream voter claims are distinct facts; a gauge kill does not by itself prove permanent loss, and a finalized round does not prove every incentive was processed. The selected venue/distributor policy must be reverified before activation, and per-incentive reconciliation runs on the existing keeper/liaison cadence. See the operations reference.
